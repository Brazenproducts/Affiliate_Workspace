Type III 550 Paracord — The Real Specification

Bartact uses genuine Type III 550 paracord in every Gladiator grab handle. Type III is the US military classification for 7-strand inner core paracord with a 550 lb minimum tensile strength rating. This is the same cord specification used in US military parachutes, and it is significantly different from the decorative paracord sold by the yard at craft stores and used by lower-quality grab handle manufacturers.

The key properties of Type III 550 paracord that matter on a grab handle are tensile strength, UV resistance, abrasion resistance, and temperature stability. At 550 lb tensile strength per strand, the cord will not fail under the lateral and downward forces applied by a passenger bracing on a trail. UV resistance means the outer sheath won't degrade from consistent sun exposure on a topless Gladiator in summer. Abrasion resistance means it handles trail grit, gloved hands, and repeated gripping without wearing through the sheath. Temperature stability means it performs consistently from freezing mountain temps to Arizona desert heat — it doesn't become brittle in cold or soften in heat.

Every color option in Bartact's lineup — all 30+ of them — uses genuine Type III 550 paracord to the same specification. There are no economy color options with lighter-weight cord. The construction is identical across all colors; only the dye changes.

Solid Steel Core — Why Bartact Handles Don't Loosen

The core of a grab handle is what determines long-term fit and stability. Generic grab handles use foam cores or hollow-formed plastic. Both of these materials compress when exposed to repeated wet-dry cycles — moisture from rain, mud, and washing gets into the foam or plastic, and when it dries, the material compresses slightly. Over dozens of cycles, the handle develops play. It starts to rock slightly on the roll bar. It rattles on the highway. The fit that seemed good at install is gone.

Bartact uses a solid steel core, custom-formed to the Gladiator JT roll bar's exact tube diameter. Steel doesn't compress. It doesn't absorb moisture and shrink. The fit you install on day one is the fit you have a year later and three years later. The handle stays locked to the roll bar — zero play, zero rattle, same grip it had when new.

The solid steel core also transmits load correctly. When a passenger braces hard through a rock crawl or severe flex, the force travels through the paracord, through the steel core, and into the roll bar mounting point. With a foam or plastic core, some of that force goes into deforming the core material — each hard use event compresses and loosens the fit a little more. With steel, 100% of the force transfers to the mounting point every time.

Bolt-On Installation — No Drilling Required
