Bartact Invented the Paracord Grab Handle

The paracord grab handle for Jeep Wrangler is a Bartact original. When Bartact engineers first built them, there was nothing like them on the market. The idea was straightforward: take the strongest, most durable hand cord used in real-world applications — US military-specification Type III 550 paracord — and wrap it around a form-fit steel core matched to the Jeep's roll bar tube diameter. The result was a grab handle that was both stronger and more visually striking than anything that existed for off-road vehicles at the time.

What followed was a market full of copies. The concept worked, so competitors built their own versions. But the copies cut corners in the same three places: they used decorative paracord (lighter weight, lower tensile rating, sold under the paracord name without the Type III 550 specification), foam or hollow-plastic cores (materials that compress with repeated wet-dry cycling and develop play over time), and universal-fit geometry (cores that approximate multiple vehicle profiles rather than matching one precisely). The result: handles that look similar in packaging and fail on trail.

Type III 550 Paracord — Why Specification Matters

The "550" in 550 paracord refers to the minimum tensile strength rating: 550 pounds per strand. Type III is the US military classification that specifies the 7-strand inner core construction that achieves and maintains that rating. This is the same cord specification used in US Army and USMC parachute suspension systems — the application that gives paracord its name.

Tensile strength is the specification that matters most in a grab handle application. When a passenger braces against lateral and downward G-forces on a technical trail — rock crawling, side-hill traverse, severe flex — the load is transferred from the hand through the paracord to the roll bar mounting point. At 550 lb tensile strength per strand, with multiple wraps of cord across the handle, there is an enormous safety margin above the forces a human passenger can generate. Decorative paracord (sometimes called "para cord" or "paracord-style") carries no tensile strength specification and is not built to the same 7-strand Type III construction. It is lighter, cheaper, and not appropriate for load-bearing applications.

UV resistance is the second key property. A topless JK in summer sun for several seasons exposes the grab handles to significant UV radiation. Type III 550 paracord uses UV-resistant nylon in both the outer sheath and inner strands. The outer sheath retains its structural integrity and color under extended UV exposure. Lighter decorative cord degrades under UV and becomes brittle — at which point it is no longer a safety-acceptable handle material.

Temperature stability is the third property. A JK that goes from Arizona desert heat to Utah mountain cold in a single weekend sees a wide temperature range. Type III 550 maintains its tensile strength and flexibility through that range. It does not become brittle in cold or soften and stretch in heat.

Solid Steel Core — Why the Handle Stays Tight

The core of a grab handle determines whether it stays tight to the roll bar over time. Bartact uses a solid steel core formed to the JK's round-tube roll bar diameter. This is the specification that keeps Bartact handles from developing play — the rocking, rattling looseness that develops in handles with foam or hollow-plastic cores after months of use.

The failure mechanism of foam and plastic cores is wet-dry cycling. The JK's interior is exposed to rain, water crossings, washing, and ambient humidity. When moisture gets into a foam core and then dries, the foam compresses slightly. Over dozens of wet-dry cycles, the compression accumulates — the core becomes slightly smaller than the tube it's clamped to. The handle develops a small amount of play. The play causes rocking. The rocking causes further compression on each load cycle. Within a year of regular trail use, a foam-core handle on a round-tube application is noticeably loose and potentially unsafe as a bracing point.

A solid steel core doesn't compress. Steel is not hygroscopic — it doesn't absorb moisture and doesn't change dimension when it does. The fit you install on day one — steel core matched to JK tube diameter — is the fit you have a year later. The handle stays locked. No play, no rattle, no degradation of the grip interface between handle and roll bar.
